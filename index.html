Overview

New text-to-audio models appear every month. Judging them still takes human listeners.

Models like AudioLDM and Tango can turn a sentence into sound, but checking whether the sound actually matches the sentence still requires slow, costly listening tests. The standard automatic score, CLAPScore, agrees with people only weakly.

The XACLE 2027 challenge asks for a model that predicts the average human rating, from 0 to 10, of how well a clip matches its prompt. This edition tests on audio from generation systems that never appear in training.

How HUM works

Step 1

Hear

Frozen Qwen2.5-Omni and M2D-CLAP encoders turn the clip and prompt into features, cached once.

Step 2

Understand

A Qwen2.5-Omni audio-language model, fine-tuned with QLoRA, answers: does this clip contain the sounds described?

Step 3

Match

A gating network combines that answer with CLAP similarity, and a score head predicts the 0–10 rating.

Research focus

Judge the sound against the prompt, not the system that made it.

Scoring models can quietly learn what familiar generators sound like, then stumble on new ones. During training, HUM compares clips that were generated for the same prompt. Because the prompt is shared, the only way to rank them correctly is to judge how well each clip carries out the text.

To test this, we hold out one generator at a time and measure how much accuracy drops on the audio the model has never heard. Our goal is a smaller drop than a strong CLAP-based baseline, backed by bootstrap confidence intervals.
